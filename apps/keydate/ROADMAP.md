# KeyDate — 1 / 3 / 5 Year Roadmap

Systematic (product, users, team) and financial (revenue, costs, funding) growth
plan. **All figures are planning assumptions, not forecasts** — they exist to be
argued with and replaced by real data. Base case shown; bear/bull noted at each
horizon. Currency CAD.

**Position today (Month 0):** working product live at keydate.ca, free-tier
infrastructure, ~0 real users, $0 revenue, solo founder, pre-incorporation.

---

## YEAR 1 — Prove people actually change behaviour
*Theme: earn the right to raise. Stop building, start measuring.*

### Q1 — Legitimacy & closed beta
- **Product:** privacy policy + terms (blocking gap — you collect income/savings
  today with neither). Email-verified signup. Support/contact channel. "Plan B"
  paths when the app says *don't buy* (see Concerns). Kill the passcode gate for
  real testers.
- **Users:** 50–100 hand-recruited beta testers. Real ones — friends-of-friends,
  Reddit r/PersonalFinanceCanada, first-time-buyer Facebook groups.
- **Ops:** incorporate (federal or provincial), business bank account, cap table.
- **Spend:** ~$2–6K (incorporation $200–1,500, legal templates $1–3K, Supabase
  Pro ~$25/mo).

### Q2 — Instrument and iterate
- **Product:** analytics on the north-star (*% logging a deposit in 3+ distinct
  months*), activation funnel, weekly cohorts. Replace seeded forum content with
  real posts. Plaid **sandbox → production** for real balances.
- **Users:** 500–1,000 registered. Target ≥25% 3-month retention.
- **Money:** apply to **non-dilutive** Canadian sources — Futurpreneur (young
  entrepreneur loan + BDC match), regional innovation programs, university/
  startup competitions, **SR&ED** credits on eligible dev work.
- **Spend:** ~$5–12K cumulative (Plaid dev ~$0–500/mo, infra, tools).

### Q3 — First distribution conversation
- **Product:** post-purchase lifecycle v1 (renewal reminders) to attack the churn
  paradox. City/cohort benchmarks — the first visible output of the data flywheel.
- **Business:** pitch **3–5 credit unions / challenger banks**. Goal: one signed
  **LOI or paid pilot** ($10–50K). Credit unions are the right first door —
  shorter sales cycles than the Big Five, and they're losing young members.
- **Users:** 2,000–3,000 registered, 600–1,000 MAU.

### Q4 — Raise or stay lean
- **Milestone gate:** ≥1,000 activated users, ≥30% 3-month retention, 1 LOI.
  *Hit it →* raise **pre-seed $250–750K** (angels, Canadian pre-seed funds,
  accelerators). *Miss it →* stay lean, keep iterating; do not raise on vanity.
- **Team:** founder + 1 part-time/contract engineer (removes bus-factor risk).

**Year 1 exit state (base):** 3,000 registered · ~1,000 MAU · $0–25K revenue ·
$20–60K spent · funded by grants/competitions or a small pre-seed · team 1–2.
*Bear:* 500 users, weak retention, no raise → pivot or park.
*Bull:* 8,000 users + paid pilot → raise at a real valuation.

## YEAR 3 — Turn engagement into contracted revenue
*Theme: a real company with real customers.*

### Product
- **B2B platform:** white-label/co-branded deployment, partner admin dashboard,
  SSO, SLAs, security review (SOC 2 readiness if enterprise deals demand it).
- **Consumer depth:** full post-purchase lifecycle (renewal, HELOC, renovation,
  next home), advanced scenarios, document vault.
- **Data flywheel visible:** "you're saving faster than 78% of Calgary buyers" —
  benchmarks no competitor can replicate without your cohort.
- **Live market data** replacing estimates (CREA/board licence or data vendor).

### Users & market
- 30,000–60,000 registered · 10,000–20,000 MAU.
- Channels: organic/community, partner-driven acquisition, content SEO on
  Canadian first-home topics.

### Financial
- **Revenue: $400K–1.2M ARR** — 2–4 B2B contracts ($75–350K/yr each) + disclosed
  referral revenue (FHSA/mortgage) + early premium tier.
- **Costs: $900K–1.6M/yr** — team of 6–10 (2–4 eng, 1 BD, 1 compliance/ops,
  1 content/community), infra $2–8K/mo, data licensing $20–80K/yr, legal/compliance.
- **Funding: Seed $1.5–3M** raised in Yr 2 on Yr-1 retention + first contract.
- **Burn:** funded, deliberate. Gross margin should be software-like (>75%) —
  watch data-licensing and support costs, which are the margin killers here.

**Year 3 exit state (base):** ~45K registered · ~15K MAU · ~$700K ARR · team ~8 ·
18+ months runway.
*Bear:* one contract, $150K ARR, extend runway, cut burn.
*Bull:* a Big-Five or national credit-union network deal → $2M+ ARR, raise Series A early.

## YEAR 5 — Category position or exit
*Theme: be the default first-home layer in Canada — or be acquired by one.*

### Product & market
- The **housing-money platform**, not a calculator: plan → save → qualify → buy →
  own → renew → next home.
- **Expansion (pick one, don't do both):**
  - *Horizontal:* UK (LISA) / Australia (FHSS) — same engine, new program rules.
  - *Vertical:* deeper into the Canadian mortgage value chain (brokerage
    partnership, or become a licensed brokerage — real revenue, real regulation).
- 150,000–400,000 registered · 50,000–120,000 MAU.

### Financial
- **Revenue: $4M–12M ARR** — majority from enterprise/B2B contracts, plus
  referral and premium.
- **Costs: $4M–9M/yr** — team 20–35.
- **Funding:** Series A ($8–15M) raised in Yr 4; approaching **default-alive**
  (profitable or clear path) by end of Yr 5 — the strongest possible position,
  because it makes every subsequent decision voluntary.
- **Outcomes:** (a) strategic acquisition — banks/fintechs buy engaged young
  customer bases and the tech to serve them; (b) keep compounding as an
  independent category leader; (c) Series B to fund international scale.

**Year 5 exit state (base):** ~250K registered · ~75K MAU · ~$7M ARR · team ~25 ·
near breakeven with an acquisition path.
*Bear:* $1–2M ARR plateau → acqui-hire or lifestyle business.
*Bull:* $15M+ ARR, category-defining, competitive acquisition interest.

## The gates that actually matter
Each is a go/no-go. Do not proceed on hope.

| Gate | Deadline | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| **Behaviour change is real** | Month 9 | ≥30% of activated users log deposits in 3+ distinct months |
| **Someone will pay** | Month 12 | 1 signed LOI or paid pilot |
| **It's a business, not a project** | Month 24 | ≥$250K ARR contracted |
| **It scales** | Month 36 | CAC payback <12 months; net revenue retention >100% on B2B |
| **It's durable** | Month 60 | Default-alive, or a credible acquisition process |

## Financial discipline notes
- **Stay non-dilutive as long as possible.** Canadian grants, SR&ED, and
  competitions are real money that costs no equity. Raise when capital
  *accelerates* a proven loop, not to discover one.
- **Watch the three margin killers:** data licensing, media storage/bandwidth
  (video especially), and human support.
- **Model the churn paradox explicitly** — if LTV is capped at the purchase
  event, the post-purchase lifecycle isn't a nice-to-have, it's the business.
- **Never raise on waitlist size.** Raise on a retention curve.
